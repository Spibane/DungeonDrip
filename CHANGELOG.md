# Changelog

### v0.17.2 - 2026-08-06
- **Ready to buy** now says when the collection behind it cannot be trusted, above the list rather than below it. Every row there is a "not owned" claim, and that is the half of a snapshot which goes wrong - the plugin already ambers a red x rather than a grey tick for exactly this reason, since glamours are added far more often than they are removed. So the one list made entirely of that claim was the one making it silently
- Four cases are called out: no Glamour Dresser data at all, a dresser snapshot older than the warning threshold, an Armoire that has never been read, and the Armoire's age when it has been. The Armoire gets a line of its own rather than being folded into the dresser's, because the two are read on different occasions and either can be the stale one alone
- That last line comes from a real report: a piece sitting in the Armoire was offered for sale because the cached Armoire read predated it, with a timestamp recent enough to look trustworthy. Opening the Armoire fixed it, and the list now says to do that instead of leaving it to be guessed

### v0.17.1 - 2026-08-05
- Fixed achievement rewards appearing in **Ready to buy** at their re-purchase price. Field Commander's Coat is granted by *Between a Rock and a Hard Place V* and carries one 1,000 gil shop row, which is the counter that hands it back to whoever earned it - so the list was offering it to characters who cannot buy it at any price. All 94 achievement-reward pieces were affected
- The cause was a rule applied to one of the two indexes and not the other. The per-piece answer already dropped the gil route in favour of naming the achievement; the by-currency answer kept it, behind a comment of mine reasoning that a character who earned the achievement really can buy it back. True, and beside the point - the counter will not sell to anyone who has not, and whether it has been earned is per-character state the sheets cannot answer. Every priced route to an achievement reward is now gated on the same setting as the other sell-back counters, and the display continues to name the achievement instead

### v0.17.0 - 2026-08-05
- **The Collection view is three tabs rather than a stack of collapsing headers.** Four headers one under another, each with its own collapsibles inside it, gave no reading of the window that told a section apart from a group within one - every list looked like a row in the list above it. A tab strip says outright that these are separate questions. The window reopens on whichever tab was last used, as it already reopens on whichever half of the window was last used
- The collapsible headings inside those tabs are no longer tinted blue. That colour was there to tell one section from the next while all four were stacked in one column, and with a tab strip doing that job a coloured heading only competes with the amber that means a stale snapshot. Amber stays where it is a warning; blue stays on the rows and figures where it marks something out rather than merely separating it
- The dresser tab holds two lists on purpose: how full the box is, and which spare copies could go. Those are one errand from both ends - the first says the box is filling and what would free space, the second is the gear that could actually be got rid of - so splitting them would put a problem on one tab and its lever on another
- **Added "Ready to buy" to the Collection view**: one group per currency held, listing the unowned gear it buys, affordable first, with the balance and an affordable count on the heading. This is the question the v0.16.0 work could not answer - that one takes a piece and gives its price, where a collector with 2,340 Poetics wants it the other way round
- **A currency is defined as whatever is in the Currency tab**, read live. That is not a shortcut but the finding that made the feature possible: the shops sheet prices gear in 2,919 different things and 2,530 of them buy exactly one piece, because most are not currencies at all but gear being traded up - 328 sit in the *Body* category, 318 in *Legs*, 296 in *Feet*. Grouping on "whatever the shop charges" would have produced two and a half thousand groups of one. Filtering by item category was measured as an alternative and does not separate them either, since *Miscellany* alone mixes 246 real currencies and one-off materials. The cost is a stated limit: a currency held at zero is absent, so this answers what can be spent rather than what farming something would be worth
- **An "Outfit sets only" toggle on the section**, and in Settings, for collecting whole outfits rather than single pieces. It earns its place on the numbers: only 14% of priced gear belongs to an outfit set, and for gil it is 483 pieces of 5,690, so the unfiltered list is mostly things somebody working through outfits will never want. Whole groups disappear as well as rows - Sack of Nuts prices 450 pieces and not one is in a set. Off by default, like every other filter, and the tooltip points anyone after one specific piece at the by-name lookup instead
- The toggle sits on the section as well as in Settings, because it is a filter on the list in front of you and a trip to Settings to narrow that is a trip nobody makes twice - the same argument as the duty window's toolbar toggles
- **Gear the Calamity Salvager or the Recompense Officer stocks is left out, by default.** Those two restock seasonal and event gear for a character who took part at the time, and the sheets record the stock without the entitlement - so 337 pieces were being offered at a price most characters cannot pay at any balance. This is the one filter in the plugin that defaults to on, because it corrects the list rather than narrowing it; off is still offered for anyone who did play through those events and really can buy them
- **A buy-back no longer reads as a way to obtain a piece.** It used to say "Vendor - 345 gil", which is a true price and a false answer - the counter will only hand the thing back to whoever earned it. Where a real route exists the real route is now what shows: Augmented Ironworks Helm of Fending reads **Special Shop - 1 Ironworks Helm of Fending**, the upgrade trade, and 712 of the 716 such pieces have one. The 4 that do not read **Re-purchase - 600 gil**, keeping the price and losing the claim
- The sell-back setting now reaches those descriptions as well as the Ready to buy list, unevenly and on purpose. With it on, the 4 pieces whose only route is a bare gil buy-back read "source unknown" everywhere; the 337 event pieces keep their line, since hiding it would have a lookup answer "source unknown" about a Pumpkin Head. Every surface that describes a piece now goes through one accessor, so a fourth or fifth cannot honour the setting by accident or miss it by accident
- Event stock is named apart from an ordinary buy-back, because the two are different news. 337 pieces read **Event re-purchase**, which says the piece is gone unless the character was there at the time; **Re-purchase** says to look for the real route on the line beneath. Neither says "Vendor" any more
- That required splitting one flag into two. Whether a route is a buy-back is a fact about the route, and the answer is "prefer the other one"; whether a piece is event stock is a fact about the piece, and the answer is "there is no other one". Holding both in one boolean is why an event piece could be reported through the creepy peddler's own shop as though that were an ordinary vendor
- **Any special shop whose only cost is gil is a buy-back too, and goes on the same setting.** Another 716 pieces, and where "Augmented Ironworks Bracelet of Casting - 280 gil" was coming from: Rowena's representatives - Auriana, Aelina, Cihanti, Aymark, Enna, Hismena - re-sell for small change what only tomestones earn. 712 of the 716 carry the real route beside it, an upgrade trade of the base piece plus a 100 gil fee, so hiding the gil listing loses nothing. An earlier note attributing these shops to the Calamity Salvager and the junkmonger was wrong and has been corrected: they are the tomestone vendors
- Gil as *one of several* costs is the opposite case and deliberately not caught, since that is the upgrade fee itself. It never read as a gil price anyway - the first cost listed is the piece being handed in, and gil is not first in any of the 3,589 multi-cost trades for storable gear
- **For the event counters, being in their stock is enough on its own, whatever else lists the piece, and in every currency.** Two readings of that were tried and both leaked. Requiring *every* route to be a sell-back route let 102 pieces through, the ones an event vendor also lists. Then scoping the mark to one currency let 156 through - excluded under gil, still offered for a Faire Voucher. It is now a property of the piece, recorded before the duplicate test rather than after: that test compares kind, label, price and currency, and a piece stocked by both a counter and an event vendor at the same gil price differs in none of them, so whichever route arrived second was discarded - losing the mark whenever that was the restricted one. This is what left Pristine Egg Cap on the list. Those other shops belong to the events themselves: the creepy peddler, the Starlight supplier, the House Valentione maid, the Moonfire Faire vendor, the Little Lady, the uma and hitsuji shonin, none of whom are in the world outside their fortnight. Every one of the 14 distinct vendors involved was checked and not one is year-round, so a second listing is evidence the piece is seasonal rather than evidence it is not
- Those vendors are found by NPC row id and their shops resolved from them, rather than by name or by a hardcoded shop list. Names are localised and would match nothing on a non-English client; a fixed list of the fifteen shop rows would go stale the first time a patch gave one of them new stock. This is also the one place the NPC-to-shop chain is used, and it works here for the reason it failed when the vendor's identity was wanted for every shop: going from a known NPC to its shops is a list lookup, where the reverse has to survive indirection that often leads nowhere
- The exclusion is applied when the list is drawn rather than when the index is built, since the index is built once per load and never invalidated - a setting baked into it could not be switched off without a reload
- Crystals are excluded for the same reason in reverse - shards do price 1,141 pieces, which would be one of the largest groups, and nobody thinks of them as money
- Gil starts collapsed, since it prices some 5,000 storable pieces, more than every other currency together. That needed a second remembered-heading list: a heading that opens by default records being shut, one that starts shut records being opened, and storing both in one list would make an absent entry ambiguous
- Each group shows its ten cheapest behind a **Show all** toggle, matching how the sets list is capped. Wolf Marks alone buy 779 pieces, and the plugin has no list clipper anywhere, so a cap with a toggle is the established answer rather than a new mechanism
- The acquisition index now carries the currency's item row and the amount alongside the rendered price. Grouping or comparing against a balance was impossible before: the only price it kept was a display string, and a translated one, so it would have grouped on the client's language
- **That index is now two indexes over one sweep, under opposite rules, and the reason is written at the line.** The per-piece answer stays collapsed to one line per kind - what stopped eight near-identical "Special Shop" lines burying a seal price - while the by-currency answer keeps every route, or a piece buyable with either tomestones or seals would only appear under one of them. Merging them would silently break whichever guarantee the merge dropped
- A piece every Grand Company sells now appears under all three seals rather than none. Giving those 19 pieces no typed price, which was the first attempt, dropped them out of the list entirely
- A piece appears once per currency, at the price that currency can be expected to take it for. The same till commonly lists one piece twice, and 323 duplicate rows would otherwise have been drawn, each stating a price nobody would choose to pay
- **MGP is the exception and quotes the dearer of its two prices**, because the cheaper one is the Make It Rain campaign - a fortnight a year - and the sheet marks neither. The data is what identifies it rather than the claim being taken on trust: across all 158 pieces MGP prices twice the discount is a consistent 30%, ratios of 0.667, 0.7 and 0.714. Nothing else in the sheet looks like that, so the exception stays a list of one: gil's duplicates run 0.283 to 1.0 with no pattern, Resistance Tokens differ by exactly three times, which is a tier rather than a sale, and the raid tokens sit at 0.375 and 0.625, which is a weapon against an accessory

### v0.16.0 - 2026-08-05
- **Any piece of gear can now be looked up, and the answer says where it comes from.** `/dungeondrip item <name>` used to only recognise gear the loot data knew a dungeon drop for - a few thousand pieces - so a crafted or bought piece could not even be named, let alone answered about. The name index now covers every piece that can be kept as a glamour, close to 29,000, and beside the drop line the answer lists the other routes: crafted, sold by a vendor, traded at a special shop, a Grand Company quartermaster, a quest reward or an achievement reward, each with what it costs
- All of that is read out of the game's own Excel sheets rather than downloaded, so it needs no network, cannot go stale, and is exact to the installed patch. It is also complete in the opposite direction from the loot data: the sheets are exhaustive about recipes and shops but know nothing of the Mog Station, seasonal events, PvP series, deep dungeons, treasure maps or relic steps. Nothing therefore says a piece cannot be obtained - the answer is **Source unknown**, with *untradable* or *try the market board* after it where the item row settles that much. With the setting off it says nothing of the sort, since a question that was never asked cannot have come back empty
- Every line reads the same way, `Route - detail`: **Crafted - Weaver Lv. 90**, **Vendor - 2,000 gil**, **Special Shop - 500 Wolf Mark**, **Grand Company - 1,060 Storm Seals**, **Quest - Sap for Smiles (Lv. 5)**, **Achievement - Let the Bodies Hit the Floor**. Prices name their own currency because the route name cannot carry the distinction: most of what the special-shop sheet describes is an item-for-item exchange rather than a currency purchase, so "Special Shop" alone would read identically for a tomestone counter and an upgrade hand-in
- **Grand Company routes name the actual seal** - Storm, Serpent or Flame - because nearly all that stock is company-exclusive: of the 464 storable pieces a quartermaster sells, 445 come from exactly one company and only 19 from all three. A generic "Grand Company seals" was hiding a real restriction on 96% of them. The 19 sold everywhere keep the generic wording, since naming one company there would invent a restriction instead. Prices never differ between companies, so one line serves either way
- Currency names agree in number with the amount, taken from the game's own plural column rather than by appending an "s" - "375 Allagan tomestones of philosophy" inflects in the middle, and the column is already translated where a rule written in the plugin would only be right in English
- A shop charging gil is reported as a vendor whatever sheet describes it - the Calamity Salvager and the junkmonger buyback counters are modelled as special shops, and 716 storable pieces come through that way, which would otherwise have given one transaction two differently worded answers
- **One line per kind, not one per route.** Listing every route buried the useful ones: Artisan's Spectacles is handed over for any of eight different materials, so eight near-identical **Special Shop** lines pushed the one line anybody wanted - that it also costs 5,000 seals - off the end. Of the 18,707 pieces answered, 13,737 now have one line, 3,792 two and 1,178 three
- **A gil route is suppressed where an achievement route exists**, because an achievement reward can be re-bought from a vendor once claimed and the sheets record that as an ordinary sale - so every such piece looked purchasable outright. This is exact rather than a guess: all 94 achievement-reward pieces in the sheet carry a gil route. Filtering instead on the suspiciously round prices those counters charge was tried and rejected after measuring, since 704 pieces with no achievement behind them sit at exactly 1,000 gil and would have lost a real vendor route
- Naming a piece no longer waits for the loot download. The name index reads only the Item sheet, so it is ready in the constructor and a piece can be named on the first frame after login and with the network down; only the drop line depends on the dataset
- Gear searches are ranked rather than alphabetical, which the widening made necessary. "ring" matches 2,770 pieces and the 15 actually named "Ring of ..." would have sorted wherever the alphabet put them and fallen off the end of the cap; matches now come back exact first, then names beginning with the query, then names containing it. A query that is the start of exactly one name resolves to it instead of listing
- Too many matches now reports how many there were rather than "more than 8", since knowing a query caught 12 pieces and not 2,770 is what decides between typing three more letters and starting again
- The right-click menu's **Where does this drop?** became **Where does this come from?**, listing the bought and crafted routes under the duties. Duties stay clickable and lead, since choosing one pins it and opens the window, where a vendor has nowhere to be taken to. The window tooltips and the collection view's missing pieces answer from the same index, so no surface can disagree with another
- **Added a link out to a reference site**, on the right-click menu for any piece and at the end of a chat lookup, with six choices under Settings. Teamcraft, Garland Tools and Universalis are addressed by item ID and always land on the right page; the two wikis and the Lodestone go by article title, which is a guess that an oddly titled piece can miss - which is why the default is Teamcraft, and why the combo says which scheme each site uses. The link is labelled with the site's bare name, since a coloured hoverable name in chat is already recognisable as a link to anyone running plugins
- The link's command ID is the item's own ID, so a chat line stays correct however many lookups follow it, and changing the site re-points every link already on screen. A fixed pool of IDs cycled round was written first and was wrong in a way worth recording: the ninth lookup reused the first slot, so clicking the oldest visible line opened whatever had overwritten it - the exact failure the pool existed to prevent, merely postponed by eight
- Reading every recipe, shop, quest and achievement in the game takes about 170ms, so it is done the first time something asks rather than during login, and not at all while the setting is off - the switch exists to skip the work, not to hide the result
- **The settings window now scrolls and stays inside the screen.** It was set to auto-resize, which grows a window to whatever its tallest tab needs and never gives it a scrollbar, so the Data tab ran off the bottom on smaller displays. Shortening that tab could not have fixed it: it lists one row per retainer read, and a character may have ten, so its height is not something the plugin can bound. Each tab now scrolls in its own right - the tab strip stays put rather than being carried off the top edge - and the window is capped to nine tenths of the screen, measured per frame so resizing the game is followed
- The two blocks on the Data tab that were not settings moved to a **Files** tab of their own: the config folder path, and the one button that empties every cache. That tab had grown to seven sections and these were the two that could leave without splitting a subject in half. Each cache keeps its own reset on the Data tab beside its refresh, since a reset only makes sense next to the thing it resets
- Gathering, fishing and retainer ventures are deliberately not among the sources. Mining, botany, fishing and spearfishing were measured against the sheets and yield no equippable gear whatsoever, so a reader for them would report nothing forever. Ventures do turn up 358 pieces, but 357 of those come from Quick Exploration, which is random and gated on the retainer's level, and every one of them is obtainable some other way that can actually be aimed at - a source line has to be something to act on

### v0.15.1 - 2026-08-04
- Fixed the panel beside the Glamour Dresser leaving out a piece the strict outfit rule says is not owned. With "owned only if every outfit with that piece is stored" on, a piece held inside one of its two sets is not collected - every other surface marks it so, and the panel beside the Armoire listed it - but the add list was asking a flatter question, whether the thing is physically in a box at all, and one stored set answered yes. The two settings look alike and are not: the storage scope narrows which box is being asked about, and this question is about every box, while the outfit mode is a rule about what counts as having a piece and the add list is the list that rule exists to change
- Rows put there by that rule say so, because they are the ones that read as a bug: the dresser is visibly holding the piece inside a set, through the window that is open. The hover names how many of the sets using it are stored and that storing the loose copy on its own settles it
- Under the strict rule such a piece also leaves the Collection view's **Already in your collection** list, which is the other half of the same fix - it was offering a spare copy for disposal while the rest of the plugin called the piece missing

### v0.15.0 - 2026-08-04
- **Added a panel beside the Armoire**, listing held gear the Armoire has not got - the counterpart of the one beside the Glamour Dresser. The gap it fills is in the game's own screen rather than in the plugin: **Store an item** lists what the Armoire *accepts*, which is a fact about the item and not about the character, so a piece already deposited sits in that list looking exactly like one that is not and the refusal comes only on the attempt, one piece at a time. The header says how many of the pieces on the character the Armoire already has, which is the count that screen will not give. That screen only: the Armoire's own window is a list of what is in there already and answers that by itself
- That panel has no fullness header and no snapshot warning, and neither is missing by accident. The Armoire has no capacity - what it accepts is decided by the game's Cabinet sheet - so which pieces to put in is never a choice between them, and its contents are read live in front of the player, which makes the dresser's age a caveat about data this list does not consult. Warning there would only teach the warning to be ignored on the panels that mean it
- It waits for the Armoire's flags to have been read since the window opened, not merely for the window, and rows say "the Armoire has not got this" rather than "the Armoire will take it from here". The flags arrive on request and read as "nothing stored" until they do, so a list built a moment early would claim the whole wardrobe was still to put in - at exactly the moment somebody is standing there ready to act on it. Whether the Armoire reaches into the armoury chest is not readable from outside it, so no row claims either way
- The two toolbar buttons for gear that is spoken for - armoury-chest gear and gear being worn - are one setting across both store panels rather than a pair each, since gear a gearset is using is using it whichever box is being stood at. Both settings sections show them and say so
- Fixed "the Armoire would take this" appearing on a piece the Armoire already had. That was read off where the piece is, which takes the first answer that holds and puts the Dresser ahead of the Armoire - the right answer to "where is it" and the wrong one to "does the Armoire have it", so a piece in both boxes was offered a home it already had. It now asks the Armoire directly
- The headings inside **Already in your collection** collapse now, one per place, and stay collapsed between sessions like the duty list's do. A full armoury chest is dozens of rows, and it sat above the retainer headings - so the one question that list exists to answer, which retainer has the spare copy, was several screens below the answer nobody was looking for. The count stays on the heading while it is shut, and it is kept out of the remembered state so collecting a piece does not reopen what was closed
- Ruled the settings tabs into their sections. Each block's heading and the lines qualifying it are muted text of the same colour, so where one subject ended and the next began was a matter of counting blank lines; a rule above each heading says it instead. Two blocks gained a heading in the process - the In-game UI tab now names its two halves, right-click menus and item tooltips, rather than running four lines together

### v0.14.0 - 2026-08-03
- **Added retainers to what the plugin can see.** A retainer's bags only load while that retainer is open, so they are snapshotted on a visit and answered from the cache afterwards - one snapshot per retainer, the same arrangement the Glamour Dresser and the Armoire have always had. A retainer that has never been opened is not counted rather than counted as empty, so on a character who has never been to a bell nothing changes at all
- **Gear a retainer is wearing is counted separately from their bags, and is off even with retainers on.** It is not in their bags, so counting it together with them is a true statement that names the wrong place - seven pages searched for a coat the retainer had on. It also has a job where it is, since a retainer's item level decides what their ventures bring back. Switch it on under the retainer setting and every list names it for what it is: "worn by one of your retainers", its own marker - a figure in a tie rather than the bags' plain figure - and "Retainer, worn" on the game's tooltip line, which is the one place the wording is spelled out rather than shortened
- **A retainer is somewhere gear is held, not somewhere it is stored.** Nothing outside the Dresser and the Armoire can be worn as a glamour, so a piece with a retainer is one that is owned and not put away - the same footing as one in a bag. Counting it is therefore opt-in like the bags are, and the two now sit under one heading on the General tab reading "also count gear you are only holding, which cannot be worn as a glamour until it goes in a box". With it on, every list obeys it: the duty list, the three panels, the loot-roll companion, the roulette advice, the sets in progress, and the game's own tooltip line, which reads `Retainer` beside the same orange diamond a carried piece gets
- The retainer market is deliberately not read. Gear listed for sale can sell while nobody is there, and a cached snapshot of it would go wrong in the one direction that matters - calling off the hunt for a piece that is no longer owned. The seven bag pages and whatever the retainer is wearing are what get read
- The Collection view's **Already in your collection** list now covers retainers, with a heading per retainer rather than one for the lot, and the bags kept apart from what the retainer is wearing: **In Ysayle's bags** and **Worn by Ysayle** are different errands, and the whole difficulty with a spare copy at a bell is knowing which one it is. They appear there whether or not retainers are being counted as owning a piece, exactly as the bags do - that setting decides what makes a piece collected, while this list is about the holdings themselves either way. The panel beside the Glamour Dresser is unchanged and still lists only what is on the character, since its subject is what can go into the box being stood at
- Fixed the confusing heading in that list. Every heading there answers "where is this" - bags, armoury chest, saddlebag, one per retainer - except **Only inside a stored outfit**, which answered "how is this accounted for" instead and sat below the others as though it were one more place. A piece in the armoury chest that only a stored outfit accounts for appeared under a heading that never said armoury, so there was no way to tell where it actually was. The list is now on one axis: the heading is the place, and each row says how the collection accounts for the piece - in the dresser, in the Armoire, or amber for part of a stored outfit set. Nothing is lost, since the row was already carrying that sentence; the amber and the hover are what now carry the caveat: there the second copy is one filled slot of a whole outfit set rather than a piece sitting in the dresser, so it only exists for as long as the set does
- **Added per-boss attribution of drops.** The wiki's duty pages carry a drop table per boss and per treasure coffer, and those headings are now read alongside the items: a piece's tooltip names the bosses and coffers known to drop it, and the list has a third grouping - **by boss or coffer** - listing what each fight and each chest gives, in the order they are met. A piece in two coffers appears under both, because it is in both. The grouping button cycles slot → role → boss
- That attribution is only as complete as the wiki lookup for the duty, and says so rather than implying otherwise. The downloaded dataset gives a duty one flat list with nothing about where inside it anything comes from, so a duty with no lookup groups into a single "not attributed" heading with a line above the list explaining why. What is on a duty's list is unchanged either way: attribution is extra detail laid over the same items, and a page laid out without a table per boss contributes its drops exactly as before
- **Fixed three duties getting nothing at all from the wiki** - no drops and no per-boss tables - because their name belongs to something else. "Ala Mhigo" is the city the duty is fought in, "Alzadaal's Legacy" and "the Fell Court of Troia" are disambiguation pages, and each duty sits at `<name> (Duty)` with around 170 listed drops across seven bosses and coffers on it. A page that carries no drop table at all is now followed there once, since nothing cheap tells a hatnote or a disambiguation page apart from a duty page but the outcome does. Unmatched item names count as a table, so a real duty page whose spellings have drifted stays put and goes on reporting the drift rather than wandering off to look for a better article. The duties that genuinely drop no gear - the Praetorium, Castrum Meridianum, the Aetherochemical Research Facility, all tomestones and a card - pay one request that finds no page each time their fortnight is up
- A wiki lookup already sitting in the cache carries no per-boss tables, since it was parsed before they were being read - so attribution arrives as each duty's lookup next refreshes, within a fortnight, or immediately from **Settings → Data → Re-fetch this duty**. Nothing has to be cleared and no items are lost in the meantime
- **Added a reset beside every refresh on the Data tab**, and one at the bottom that does all of them: the collection snapshot for this character, the downloaded loot data, the wiki lookups and the drops learned in game. Each takes two presses, because a dresser snapshot can be weeks of visits and a row of one-click "forget" buttons is a mistake waiting for a stray click. Every one of these is disposable cache that rebuilds itself from the game or from upstream, so a reset costs time rather than anything permanent - nothing in the game is touched, and settings are not part of it, which the window says out loud since "reset everything" is exactly what somebody wanting their settings back would reach for
- Retainer snapshots taken before the bags and the worn gear were told apart are discarded on load, since the two are in one list there with no way to separate them again and keeping them means going on naming the wrong place. The Data tab will show those retainers as unread until the next visit; nothing else is affected
- Retainers can also be forgotten one at a time, which is the case that needs it: dismiss a retainer in game and nothing ever comes back to say so, leaving the name in the list and its contents counted as owned forever
- Forgetting the loot download deletes the ETags with the file, which is what makes it different from the update check beside it. That check asks upstream "changed?" and is told no, so a dataset sitting on disk wrongly could never be dislodged by it. The duty lists keep working from what is already loaded until the replacement lands, so a failed re-download does not cost the offline copy
- Changed the wikitext parse out of the lookup that fetches it and into its own file, free of Dalamud and Lumina like the ownership decision in Core. It is a parser for third-party markup that changes without warning, and it now has one job and no way to reach the game
- Every file now says why it exists. The comments here have always explained why rather than what, but a dozen types carried no summary at all, so the reason a thing exists as its own thing was recoverable only by reading it and inferring - and the three kinds of thing that are not readable off a signature were being missed: an order that is load-bearing, a shape that looks wasteful and is not, and what a return value cannot mean

### v0.13.0 - 2026-08-03
- **Added leaving out gear this character can never wear**, on the General tab, as two switches over one rule: gear locked to the other gender, and gear locked to another race. Every list obeys them - the duty list, the three panels, the loot-roll companion, the roulette advice and the sets in progress. The gender lock is the one that matters: a couple of hundred pieces carry it and 97 whole outfit sets are made of nothing else, the summer halters and tops and the Moonfire attire, and without this they sit on the missing lists forever since no amount of collecting will make them wearable. The race lock is another race's starting gear, 56 pieces of it.
